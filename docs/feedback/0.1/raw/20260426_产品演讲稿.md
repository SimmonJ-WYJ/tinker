## Step 1: Before the Video, Approximately 2-3 Minutes

Good afternoon, everyone. Today we are demonstrating ASCI Alpha.

For this version, we did not begin with an all-encompassing research assistant. Research is complex. Promising everything at the outset can easily produce a general chat tool that looks powerful but is unreliable at critical steps.

In Alpha, we therefore focus on two fundamental and frequent research scenarios: paper search and literature review.

Why these two?

Almost every research question returns to two actions: finding relevant literature thoroughly and accurately, and quickly understanding the structure, development, and gaps in existing research.

Neither is straightforward in practice.

Search is more than entering a few keywords and waiting. A research question has many expressions: synonyms, abbreviations and full names, broader and narrower concepts, interdisciplinary terminology, and database-specific language. Expanding keywords, deciding which terms to retain or exclude as noise, and balancing recall against relevance all require experience.

Many AI tools can now generate something that looks like a review. Researchers, however, want to know which papers support it, why relevance was assessed that way, why the review is structured as it is, and which conclusions have explicit sources versus model inference.

When those processes are invisible, even fluent results struggle to become part of research work.

ASCI Alpha therefore has a clear goal: support research judgment by making search and review more transparent, traceable, and suitable for researchers.

The following 5-minute video shows how ASCI starts from a research question and assists with keyword expansion, paper search, relevance assessment, and an initial review.

## Step 3: Post-Demonstration Summary, Approximately 3-4 Minutes

You have just seen ASCI Alpha's two current core capabilities: literature search around a research question and traceable review content based on the results.

Our vision for ASCI extends beyond paper search and summarization.

We want an Agent foundation for research tasks. Through tools, data sources, MCP services, and Skills, ASCI should gradually support more research scenarios. We also hope to work with the OPENSCI community to build more Agents for real research workflows on this foundation.

Next, we will focus on three directions.

First, opening the black box.

In research, users need an understandable, inspectable, and adjustable process as well as an answer.

The expanded keywords, queried sources, reasons for considering a paper relevant, and materials supporting the final review should not remain hidden inside the system.

We want ASCI to say, “Here is my work process. You can inspect and change it,” rather than merely ask users to trust an answer.

Second, topic-oriented memory.

Research is ongoing. The same topic requires repeated searches, additional literature, refined questions, and new materials.

ASCI's memory should extend beyond chat history to sustained topic context: research themes, terminology preferences, previous search results, existing literature, user concerns, and intermediate research goals.

Topic-level memory can turn ASCI from a one-off tool into a long-term research collaborator.

Third, deeper forms of research work.

Alpha uses conversational interaction for search and review because it is natural and easy to learn.

Not every research task fits chat alone. Data analysis, experimental design, paper-structure planning, and research-proposal comparison often need tables, flowcharts, visual panels, editable task chains, or continuous background Agent execution.

ASCI will retain conversation as an entry point while providing interaction forms appropriate to different research tasks.

To summarize ASCI Alpha in one sentence:

We want AI to participate in the research process transparently, traceably, and collaboratively while researchers retain responsibility for conclusions.

If you work on literature-intensive research or are interested in research Agents, open research toolchains, MCP, or collaborative Skill development, we would welcome continued discussion.
