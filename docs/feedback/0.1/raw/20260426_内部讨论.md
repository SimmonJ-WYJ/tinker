Discussion 1: Should ASCI Currently Invest in Paper Downloads, Parsing, RAG, and Web3 Resource Contributions?

Dimension	View expressed
Yannik's view	Paper downloads, subsequent parsing, and RAG (retrieval-augmented generation) are the largest current bottlenecks and consume substantial resources. Web3 mechanisms might mobilize users to contribute missing resources.
Neo's view	Do not rush to solve resource-intensive problems. Parsing data, vector databases, and RAG results for the same DOI (Digital Object Identifier) have sharing/reuse value if otherwise recreated repeatedly, but this is not the highest current priority.
Implicit judgment	Downloads and parsing are infrastructure issues, but short-term work should avoid accumulating resources indiscriminately. First identify the capabilities essential to a Science Operating System.

Interim conclusion:

Paper downloads, parsing, vectorization, and RAG-result reuse are important infrastructure, but should not receive heavy investment first.
A more reasonable sequence is to establish ASCI's core research operations, then gradually build literature resources, shared caches, and RAG-reuse mechanisms.

ASCI's strategic positioning should rise from a research-Agent tool to a research operating-system foundation.
The immediate priority is an understandable, tangible, extensible research paradigm rather than winning an isolated feature category.

One Point of Disagreement

Neo's statement that strategic resources need not go to something others will inevitably do better is directionally reasonable but easily misunderstood.

ASCI need not lead the industry in paper downloads, but it needs a sufficiently controllable literature-processing chain to support reviews, evidence sets, and Agent collaboration.

The question is therefore how much download/parsing/RAG capability can support the core experience without becoming a resource sink.

My answer:

Make it usable, reusable, and extensible, without chasing the most complete resources, strongest parsing, or first place in retrieval.

The Most Reasonable Current Positioning

ASCI is a research AI operating system entered through literature workflows, extending beyond search or review alone.
Stage one establishes perceptible value through search and review.
Stage two accumulates research objects: evidence sets, notes, citations, tasks, and Agent collaboration.
Stage three establishes reusable research-workflow foundations and an Agent runtime.

Final Recommendation

Avoid a binary divide between tool advocates and operating-system advocates.

A more precise consensus:

ASCI's strategic destination is a research AI operating system.
Its product entry point is literature search and review.
Its first defensible strengths should be research-object modeling, evidence-chain organization, multi-Agent collaboration, and project-level context memory rather than paper-resource volume.
The first stage must make literature processing sufficiently usable while avoiding an endless resource-collection and PDF-parsing battle.
